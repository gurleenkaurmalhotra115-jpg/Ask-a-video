1. Research Questions & Empirical Verdicts
  • RQ1 (Semantic vs. Fixed-Token): Dense semantic chunking fails to outperform simple fixed-token partitioning (8.5% vs 6.2% Hit@1, McNemar p = 0.508). Consistent with Qu et al. (2024).
  • RQ2 (Acoustic Robustness): Tier C (Regional Accented) achieves 30.0% Hit@4 under fixed-token, matching Tier A (34.0%), demonstrating dense semantic tolerance to non-native phonetics.
  • RQ3 (Search Space Expansion): Short lectures (≤35m) achieve 23% Hit@1, but long lectures (>35m) collapse to 3–6% Hit@1 due to lexical dispersion. Mitigated by Hierarchical Retrieval to 18% Hit@1.
  • RQ4 (Grounding & Attribution): Closed-world prompt achieves 100% negative rejection (26/26 probes), but rejects 53.3% of answerable queries when retrieval misses.

5. Algorithm 2: Two-Stage Hierarchical Video Retriever
  • Problem: In long lectures (>35 min), professors repeat core terms (*"gradient", "loss"*) across 40+ chunks. Flat dense top-1 drops to 3–6% (median boundary error ~950s).
  • Stage 1 (Macro Blocks): Partition lecture into coarse 5-min intervals ($T_{\text{macro}}=300$s). Retrieve top-$M=2$ macro blocks exhibiting highest dense cosine similarity.
  • Stage 2 (Micro Grounding): Search micro-chunks exclusively within the active 10-minute window.
  • Result: Recovers Hit@1 from 6% to 18% [95% CI: 11%, 25%] and slashes median boundary error by 74% (1012s → 264s).
