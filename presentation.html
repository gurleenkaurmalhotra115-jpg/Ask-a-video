Evaluating Chunking Strategies and Temporal Grounding in Long-Form Educational Video RAG Under Acoustic Noise

Guru Gobind Singh Indraprastha University (GGSIPU) — Capstone Research Defense

Presenter: Gurleen Kaur Malhotra

Department: Information Technology, Maharaja Surajmal Institute of Technology

Keywords: Video RAG, Temporal Grounding, MedVidQA, Acoustic Noise, NPTEL.

An empirical investigation across 13 STEM lectures (~20,000 transcript segments) validating chunking limits, search space expansion, and zero-hallucination citation constraints.

🎙️ Speaker Notes: Good morning respected evaluators. Today I present VidQuery, an open-source, timestamp-grounded video RAG framework. While standard RAG has been heavily benchmarked on written text, educational video transcripts pose unique acoustic, continuous, and lexical dispersion challenges. In this work, we conduct an empirical factorial investigation across four chunking strategies and 13 full-length lectures to test whether neural semantic chunking justifies its cost on spoken discourse.

Motivation & The Problem

Why Does Text RAG Break Down on Spoken Lectures?

  • 1. Continuous Unpunctuated ASR Streams: Unlike textbooks with clear paragraphs and headings, automated ASR transcripts lack consistent punctuation, leading to arbitrary chunking artifacts.
  • 2. Acoustic Channel & Accent Variance: Classrooms feature ambient echo, chalkboard noise, and non-native speech (e.g. Indian English in NPTEL), degrading ASR word accuracy.
  • 3. The Lexical Dispersion Dilemma: In a 2-hour lecture, core terms (gradient descent, loss, activation) appear dozens of times across intro, proofs, and recaps. Flat vector retrieval struggles to isolate the primary derivation.
  • 4. Requirement for Temporal Provenance: Students do not just want an answer; they require clickable timestamps to jump directly to the exact moment in the video.
🎙️ Speaker Notes: When applying RAG to video, clean text assumptions break down. ASR is continuous, accents degrade phonetic accuracy, and foundational keywords are repeated dozens of times throughout a 2-hour discourse, confusing flat dense similarity.

Research Questions & Prior Literature

Empirical Foundation & Objectives

  • The Qu et al. (Vectara, 2024) Finding: Qu et al. showed that embedding-based semantic chunking rarely beats simple fixed-token partitioning on text documents despite $O(N)$ transformer overhead.
    👉 RQ1: Does this premise hold true on spoken educational transcripts?
  • MedVidQA Temporal Intersection Standard (ACL 2022): Rather than binary text recall, we evaluate temporal precision using Intersection-over-Union thresholds ($R@1 \text{ @ IoU} \ge 0.3, 0.5, 0.7$).
  • RQ2 (Search Space Expansion): How does video duration (short $\le 35\text{m}$ vs. multi-hour) and non-native acoustic noise (NPTEL) impact temporal localization?
  • RQ3 (Faithfulness): Can closed-world citation constraints achieve zero hallucination on adversarial out-of-domain queries?
🎙️ Speaker Notes: We set out to empirically answer three core questions: First, does expensive semantic chunking beat simple token chunking on speech? Second, how does multi-hour video duration affect dense retrieval? And third, can we achieve zero hallucination with citation grounding?

Core Empirical Finding: Semantic chunking achieved only 3.1% Hit@1, trailing fixed-token chunking (4.6%), while incurring heavy transformer preprocessing latency. On speech transcripts, informal discourse shifts trigger false boundary cuts.
🎙️ Speaker Notes: Across 1,000 bootstrap iterations, semantic chunking underperformed simple fixed-token partitioning in Hit@1 (3.1% vs 4.6%) and MRR (0.087 vs 0.097). Spoken transcripts lack formal paragraph structures, causing semantic cosine dips to cut mathematical derivations in half.

The Search Space Expansion Effect (Table 3)

Short Lectures vs. Multi-Hour Classroom Discourses

Duration Bracket Strategy Hit@1 Hit@4 MRR Mean IoU Median Error

Key Insight: On short videos, temporal chunking reaches 23.3% Hit@1. On multi-hour lectures, chunk counts balloon to 800+, compressing flat cosine similarity and degrading top-1 precision to 3–6%. This proves the necessity of hierarchical indexing.

🎙️ Speaker Notes: On short videos, time-window chunking achieves a strong 23.3% Hit@1. But in multi-hour lectures, foundational keywords repeat across two hours, dispersing semantic similarity. This proves that long-form educational RAG requires two-stage hierarchical indexing.

Summary of Contributions & Future Roadmap

Conclusion & Architectural Next Steps

  • 1. Qu et al. Validated on Speech: Neural semantic chunking fails to justify its computational overhead on spoken discourse compared to fixed-token chunking.
  • 2. Search Space Formalization: Formalized the Search Space Expansion Effect, establishing the boundary where flat vector search degrades.
  • 3. Acoustic Noise Tolerance: Proved dense embeddings retain 30% top-4 recall on Indian-accented NPTEL classrooms.
  • 4. Zero Hallucination: Achieved 100% negative probe rejection via vector margins and citation grounding.

Future Work (Documented in FUTURE_WORK.md):

  • Two-Stage Hierarchical Indexing (Macro Chapter Partitioning + Micro Cross-Encoder Reranking).
  • Multimodal Keyframe Extraction and OCR Slide Fusion using structural similarity ($\Delta \text{SSIM}$).
🎙️ Speaker Notes: In summary, VidQuery establishes rigorous empirical baselines for video RAG. Our future work will implement two-stage hierarchical indexing and multimodal keyframe extraction to push temporal localization even further.
